Clinical Pearls: Genetic Testing, Cascade Screening & LDL-C Targets

When to Order Genetic Testing (LDLR, APOB, PCSK9, LDLRAP1)
  • Recommended in adults with Probable or Definite FH (DLCN ≥ 6), or Possible FH with a strong family history of premature ASCVD.
  • A pathogenic variant confirms the diagnosis (adding 8 points), clarifies lifelong cardiovascular risk (2–3× higher ASCVD risk at any given LDL-C compared with polygenic hypercholesterolemia), and enables definitive DNA cascade testing in relatives.
  • Negative genetic testing does not exclude clinical FH: ~20–40% of phenotypically definite FH patients have polygenic hypercholesterolemia or an unidentified variant and still require aggressive LDL-C lowering.
Cascade Screening & Pediatric Timing
  • Heterozygous FH is autosomal dominant: each first-degree relative (parent, sibling, child) has a 50% probability of inheriting the condition.
  • Screen first-degree relatives with a fasting lipid panel (and targeted variant testing if a familial mutation is identified).
  • Children: Screen starting at age 2 years if there is a family history of FH or premature ASCVD; statin therapy is typically initiated at age 8–10 years if LDL-C remains ≥160 mg/dL (or ≥130 mg/dL with additional risk factors).
