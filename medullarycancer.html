Method: For each marker, ln(concentration) is regressed on time by least squares; Doubling time = ln(2) ÷ slope. Months are calculated as days ÷ 30.4375. The ATA guideline describes fitting single exponentials by nonlinear least-squares regression, so results may differ slightly from the ATA online calculator when values fluctuate.

Data requirements: The ATA guideline states that reliable estimates require at least four data points over a minimum of 2 years, although doubling times under 6 months can be reliably estimated within the first 12 months postoperatively (Wells et al., Thyroid, 2015). Use postoperative values from the same assay, and do not combine values from before and after a change in treatment in one fit.

Prognostic context (calcitonin): In a retrospective study of 65 patients, 5- and 10-year survival was 25% and 8% with a calcitonin doubling time under 6 months and 92% and 37% with 6 to 24 months; all patients with doubling times over 2 years were alive at the end of the study (Barbet et al., Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism, 2005). A meta-analysis found the strongest separation at 1 year or less versus more than 1 year for both calcitonin and CEA (Meijer et al., Clinical Endocrinology, 2010). The 6- and 24-month bands shown for CEA are descriptive only. Both markers should be assessed; a rising CEA with stable or falling calcitonin may indicate dedifferentiation.

This tool supports, and does not replace, clinical judgment and imaging assessment.
