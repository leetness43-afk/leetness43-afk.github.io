Recommended Vitamin D Replacement

Recommended 25(OH)D Range in Osteoporosis: 30–50 ng/mL (75–125 nmol/L)

AACE/ACE 2020: maintain 25(OH)D at 30 ng/mL or higher in patients with osteoporosis, with a preferable range of 30 to 50 ng/mL.

  • Vitamin D3 1,000–2,000 IU daily is typically required to stay in range; higher doses may be needed with obesity, malabsorption, or older age.
  • Total calcium intake (diet plus supplement if needed) of 1,200 mg daily for women aged 50 years or older.
  • Correct deficiency before starting zoledronic acid or denosumab to lower the risk of hypocalcemia.
  • The upper bound of 50 ng/mL is a consensus limit; no fracture benefit has been shown above it.

Clinical Assessment Summary (Sig)

--
Clinical Note: Categories and regimens follow the 2011 Endocrine Society guideline (deficiency below 20 ng/mL, insufficiency 21–29 ng/mL, sufficiency 30 ng/mL or higher). The 2024 Endocrine Society guideline no longer endorses specific 25(OH)D thresholds and suggests against routine testing in healthy adults, so this tool applies to adults with an indication for testing (for example osteoporosis, malabsorption, or hypocalcemia). The band below 12 ng/mL is an added flag for possible osteomalacia. Not for children, pregnancy, or patients who need active vitamin D (calcitriol). Tolerable upper intake is 4,000 IU daily without supervision (National Academy of Medicine) and 10,000 IU daily under medical supervision (Endocrine Society 2011). Daily dosing is preferred for long-term maintenance; long-term large intermittent doses have been linked to more falls and fractures.
